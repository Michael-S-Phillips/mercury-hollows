# Non-volatile alternatives and the null hypothesis (Phase 3b)

**Track:** adversarial quality control on the hollows study. Before the study commits to a
volatile-loss origin for Mercury's hollows, this track tests the non-volatile alternatives and the
optical-only null, audits the 2021 Icarus referee objections, and re-screens every candidate against
the new Wang et al. (2020) age constraint. Every quantity is flagged **measured** (laboratory / catalog
data), **modeled** (this study's forward calculation), or **estimated** (a disclosed bracket). Every DOI
is Crossref-verified and listed in `docs/verified_bibliography.md`.

---

## 0. The rate bar the alternatives must clear

Two rate windows are carried side by side throughout, because the verdict is sensitive to which one applies:

- **Phillips 2021 window (Table 5):** 3.3–130 m/Gyr — the min-effective / characteriztic surface-lowering
  rate used in Phases 1–2. **modeled/estimated.**
- **Wang 2020 window:** **6.9×10⁴ – 1.6×10⁷ m/Gyr** (median/best-age 2.0×10⁵ m/Gyr). **modeled**, derived
  from required rate = depth / age with Blewett 2016 depths (median 20.8 m, max 110.5 m) and the Wang et al.
  (2020, `10.1029/2020JE006559`) crater-statistics model age of the global hollow population, **103 (+200, −96) kyr**
  (an *upper limit*; bracket 7–303 kyr). **measured (crater counts).**

The Wang window sits **~3.2 orders of magnitude above the top of the Phillips 2021 window.** Wang independently
measures real relief (average ≈24 m) and a global volatile-loss budget of **>1,266 km³** — an independent second
dataset confirming that hollows are a genuine, recent, mass-removing phenomenon. Wang's own preferred volatile is
**carbon** (graphite lost as CH₄ via solar-wind-proton reduction), a nuance carried through below.

## 1. Non-volatile formation hypotheses — each assessed against the observed morphology

The morphology any successful hypothesis must reproduce (**measured**, established in Phases 1–2): median depth
**20.8 m** (5th–95th 5.1–53.5 m, max 110.5 m; memoryless exponential tail → no self-limiting depth ceiling →
supply/age-limited), sub-km scale in fields, flat floors, sharp rims, **high reflectance**, hosted almost
exclusively on **low-reflectance material (LRM)**, on crater walls/rims/central structures, to 71.4°N (78.5°N
in the local catalog), on systematically **equator-facing** slopes (24/31 groups within 45° of due south), and
**morphologically fresh / possibly active** (Wang: <103 kyr).

| Hypothesis | Can it make 20.8 m (→110 m) relief? | Verdict | Decisive failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Electrostatic dust lofting** | Redistributes only the finest (<~10 µm) fraction; leaves a coarse armouring lag; needs an external sink for >100 m of processed column | **EXCLUDED** | No mechanism to *export* tens of m of solid; terminator-driven, contradicts equator-facing aspect; no brightening of LRM |
| **Thermal fatigue / comminution** | Breaks rock to fines but removes **no mass** (Delbo 2014, `10.1038/nature13153`) | **EXCLUDED (necessary, not sufficient)** | Preconditioning only; every coupled removal agent is ≥1 order below even the Phillips window |
| **Impact-melt drainage / void collapse** | Can make 10s of m of flat-floored relief | **EXCLUDED** | **Age**: melt solidifies within yr–kyr of impact; hollows post-date host craters and are <103 kyr — contemporaneous collapse is ruled out. No brightening; wrong distribution |
| **Bare-silicate sputter erosion** | max cusp rate **0.047 m/Gyr** (bracket, **modeled**) | **EXCLUDED** | 1.4% of the Phillips floor; **7 orders** below the Wang window; aspect-neutral (cusp-latitude driven); no brightening |
| **Micrometeoroid vaporization** | max perihelion rate **0.22 m/Gyr** (**modeled**) | **EXCLUDED** | Composition-blind; gardens (reburies) as much as excavates; ~6 orders below Wang window |
| **Optical-only (no material removal)** | Predicts **0 m** relief | **EXCLUDED (refuted)** | Measured median 20.8 m and Wang's >1,266 km³ loss budget directly refute a topography-free model |

**Quantitative core (modeled).** Summing *all* bare-surface athermal removal at its optimiztic upper bracket
(cusp sputtering + perihelion micrometeoroid vaporization) gives **0.27 m/Gyr**. Against the Wang window this is
short across the entire bracket: excavating the 20.8 m median at the best-fit 103 kyr age needs 2.0×10⁵ m/Gyr, which
this ceiling misses by **5.9 orders** (78 Gyr to excavate 20.8 m, ~17× the age of the solar system); at the extreme
bracket — the deepest hollow (110.5 m) at the shortest age (7 kyr), 1.6×10⁷ m/Gyr — the shortfall is **7.8 orders**.
Non-volatile bare-surface erosion is not marginally too slow; it is decisively excluded across the whole range.

**The reflectance requirement.** Hollows are *high-reflectance* and form *on* LRM, so any winning hypothesis must
explain the **brightening**, not merely the depression. The self-consistent Phase-1 picture holds: LRM is darkened
by **graphite** (Murchie 2015 `10.1016/j.icarus.2015.03.027`; Peplowski & Klima 2016 `10.1038/ngeo2669`), and removal
of a darkening agent (graphite and/or a co-hosted volatile) brightens the floor. Non-volatile erosion, impact-melt
exposure, and lofting produce **no systematic brightening**; the optical-only null produces brightening but **no
depression**. Only **volatile loss from within the grain-supporting fabric** does both — and, per the pore-space
analysis (below), the volatile must be **concentrated** (≥~4% of solid volume) for its removal to lower the surface
at all.

## 2. The Wang re-screen — an entire mechanism class eliminated

Re-screening the 56-candidate combined loss matrix against the Wang window (maximum rate across all nine mechanisms
at the 78.5°N cold limit) **inverts the study's headline athermal result**:

- **Every athermal-only route now fails by 3–8 orders.** Photon-stimulated desorption tops out at ~30 m/Gyr for any
  phase; the chloride **VUV-photolysis "rehabilitation"** — even at its most generous *unmeasured* quantum yield
  (56 m/Gyr) — is **3.1 orders below** the Wang requirement. This is not a loss of a result but a **stronger one**:
  *no athermal route can excavate a hollow on the observed ~100 kyr timescale*, which eliminates an entire class of
  mechanisms independent of composition.
- **Wang's own carbon→CH₄ route is also supply-limited.** Solar-wind-proton reduction of graphite to methane reaches
  only ~2 m/Gyr even at the physically impossible *unshielded* solar-wind flux ceiling — **5 orders below** the Wang
  requirement. Carbon, like sulfur, cannot be *rate*-limited; it must be **supply/exposure**-limited.
- **Fullerenes, viable under the old window, now fail** by 1.4 (C₆₀) to 2.3 (C₇₀) orders.
- **Elemental sulfur is the only candidate whose loss rate exceeds the Wang requirement** — by ~3.8 orders at the
  cold limit (7.7×10⁸ m/Gyr). The sign matters: it *exceeds* the bar, which is precisely the **supply-limited**
  regime independently identified in Phases 1–2 (a static exposed reservoir is removed essentially instantly; a
  *dynamically replenished* reservoir, Verkercke et al. 2025, sits in steady state between implantation and loss).

Twenty phases meet the Wang rate at the cold limit; of those, ices (latitude — confined to polar cold traps),
organics (radiolytic carbonisation in 125–251 Myr ≫ 103 kyr, with residue that *darkens*), native metals (no bulk
reservoir + athermal destruction), and Se/Te/P (abundance/stability) are independently excluded. **Elemental sulfur
is the single survivor of all four filters** (abundance, poleward reach, radiolytic survival, rate).

## 2a. Wang argues for carbon — we disagree, quantitatively

Wang et al. (2020, §4.1) favor **carbon** over sulfur: hollows sit in graphite-darkened LRM
(Murchie 2015; Peplowski & Klima 2016), the 500–600 nm graphite feature is *absent* on hollow floors
but present on background terrain, and they propose graphite is lost as **CH₄** by reaction with
implanted solar-wind protons (C + 4H → CH₄; Blewett 2016), the escaping CH₄ lofting the bright-halo
fines. This is in direct tension with our Phase-1 verdict (graphite = passive LRM host, fails every
route). The difference is **mechanism, not chemistry**: we screened graphite on sublimation /
photolysis / sputtering; Wang invokes **chemical reduction**, a different rate law.

**The C→CH₄ route is falsified by conservation of protons (modeled, independently verified).** The
reaction C(s) + 4H → CH₄(g) cannot consume more carbon than there are protons to react with, so the
solar-wind proton flux is a **hard ceiling** on the rate — no rate law, efficiency, or free parameter
enters. All deficits below are benchmarked against the **carbon-specific requirement of 2.3×10⁵ m/Gyr**
(Wang's 24 m mean depth ÷ 103 kyr best-fit age), matching the athermal track's convention; comparisons
to the Wang *window floor* (6.9×10⁴ m/Gyr) are ~0.5 dex smaller and flagged as such. At the **cusp-peak
proton flux** (1.03×10¹¹ m⁻² s⁻¹, the highest anywhere on Mercury), with *every* arriving proton forming
CH₄ at 4 H per C, graphite removal is **7.2×10⁻³ m/Gyr — 7.5 orders below the carbon-specific requirement**
(7.0 orders below the window floor). An independent cross-check on Wang's own global budget agrees:
removing 1,266 km³ of C as CH₄ needs **5.7×10⁴¹ protons**, but the solar wind delivers only **1.9×10³⁴**
to the 57,400 km² hollow area in 103 kyr — **7.5 orders short**, equivalent to **3,062 Gyr** at cusp-peak
flux and 100% efficiency (~220× the age of the universe). Two independent routes agreeing to 0.1 dex.
Every rescue fails: diluting the carbon helps only linearly (at the measured 1.4 wt% the rock-lowering
gap is still 5.8 dex against the carbon-specific 2.3e5 m/Gyr requirement), and even a chemically
nonsensical 1:1 H:C stoichiometry leaves 5.2 dex. (Against the Wang *median* requirement
2.02e5 these are 5.7 and 5.1 dex, and against the window floor 6.9e4 they are 5.2 and 4.6 dex;
the earlier draft quoted the median-basis values under a carbon-specific heading.) (An earlier stoichiometric-efficiency variant quoted 5.3 dex for a ~1 m/Gyr ceiling vs the Wang
*median*; the proton-supply ceiling above supersedes it and is the number to cite.)

**Our graphite elimination survives, now on the strongest possible footing** — conservation of protons,
not a modeled sublimation rate, which is far harder to contest. Frame the disagreement precisely:
**Wang's observations stand** (the graphite 500–600 nm feature is absent on hollow floors and present in
background terrain; hollows concentrate in LRM). What fails is the **loss mechanism** proposed to explain
them. The spectral evidence is equally consistent with graphite as the **passive host** removed along
with excavated material — exactly our Phase-1 assignment. (The proton-supply figures — cusp-peak ceiling 7.2×10⁻³ m/Gyr, 7.5-dex rate deficit
vs the carbon-specific requirement (7.0 dex vs the window floor), 5.7×10⁴¹ protons needed vs 1.9×10³⁴
delivered, 7.5-dex budget deficit, 3,062 Gyr — were reproduced from scratch this session and match the
inbound values exactly; the two rescue-attempt deficits (5.8 and 5.2 dex against the carbon-specific requirement; 5.7 and 5.1 on the median basis) were also
independently recomputed. The concentration requirement of §2b is the separate result in
`runs/concentration_requirement.json`.)

### 2b. The concentration requirement is an enrichment-or-depth trade-off, not a single threshold

Wang's mass balance (their eq 5) **couples** the volatile column to the hollow depth: with their own
parameters the submillimeter halo terms are negligible, so **h_volatile ≈ 0.95 h_HF at every depth**
(verified: h_volatile(24 m) = 22.9 m, reproducing their published ">22 m", an explicit *minimum* since
they set ρ_lag = ρ_bedrock). The volatile column removed is therefore fixed by the hollow depth, and the
source volume-fraction is **f = h_volatile / h_source** where **h_source — how far the source extends
below the hollow — is a free parameter.** The budget fixes only the *product* f × h_source, **not f
alone**: there is no single binding vol%. Required *f* against how deep the source runs (source depth as
a multiple of hollow depth):

| source depth | *f* (median hollow) | *f* (deepest hollow) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1× hollow depth | 94.7 vol% | 99.0 vol% |
| 2× | 47.4 vol% | 49.5 vol% |
| 5× | 18.9 vol% | 19.8 vol% |
| 10× | 9.5 vol% | 9.9 vol% |
| 20× | 4.7 vol% | 5.0 vol% |

**The honest statement:** to source the volatile at Mercury's global-average S (4 wt% = 5.4 vol%), the
reservoir must extend **~18× deeper than the hollow — about 365 m below a median 20.8 m hollow.** So
**either** the volatile is locally enriched well above the planetary average, **or** the hollow taps a
reservoir extending some hundreds of meters below it. The budget cannot distinguish these two branches;
it only forbids *both* being false. This binds *every* candidate symmetrically, **including elemental
sulfur** — sulfur passes the proton-supply test that kills the carbon route, but it faces this
enrichment-or-depth demand on equal terms, the sharpest **open question** for the surviving candidate.

**The two branches are co-equal, not requirement-plus-caveat.**
- *Local enrichment* implies a concentrating process (magmatic sulfide segregation, exhalative deposition).
- *Fracture-fed deep reservoir* implies fracture-mediated access to a deeper body — exactly the
  cooling-fracture structural control Wang document (their §3.1), and **consistent with the significant
  hollow–crater association found here (5 km z=3.4, 10 km z=4.0) and with the absence of near-field vent
  association**, which together point at impact-fracture access rather than fumarolic emplacement.

**Relation to the pore-space floor:** both constraints apply and they are different statements. The
pore-space threshold (x_v* ≈ 4 vol%, bracket 0–27%) remains a genuine independent **floor** — below it,
removal empties pores without lowering the surface. The budget is a **trade-off**, not a floor, and does
*not* supersede the pore-space threshold. The requirement is: *f* must **exceed the pore-space floor AND**
satisfy f × h_source = h_volatile. The geologic conclusion is unchanged — the former must be
**concentrated**, not dispersed trace material — but it now rests on the pore-space floor plus the
enrichment-or-depth trade-off, rather than on a single 20 vol% figure. Detail in
`runs/concentration_requirement.json`.

**The volatile-budget test sharpens it further (measured inventories).** Wang's >1,266 km³ lost in
≤103 kyr is **17.1% of the near-surface S inventory** (7,420 km³ at 4 wt% — the high-S terrane maximum, Weider et al. 2015; 3,520-4,270 km³ at the 1.9-2.3 wt% global mean, giving 29.7-35.9%) but **41.2% of
the near-surface C inventory** (3,070 km³ at 1.4 wt%, Peplowski 2015). Without replenishment, the loss
rate exhausts the S reservoir in ~0.60 Myr and the C reservoir in ~0.25 Myr — both ≪ Mercury's age,
so **active rejuvenation is required** (Wang's own conclusion), which independently supports the
supply-limited / replenished picture reached from the depth distribution. Sulfur has an established
replenishment mechanism (Verkercke et al. 2025, exospheric/ionic implantation); carbon does not.
**Carbon therefore faces both a ~5-order supply-rate deficit and an inventory demand twice as severe
as sulfur's, with no known recharge.** (Depth basis: Wang uses a 24 m *average* over 57,400 km²,
Thomas 2014; we use the 20.8 m *median* from the Blewett n=2,518 point catalog — both reported, not
mixed. The 103 kyr is an *upper limit* from a single candidate impact crater in all <10 m/px NAC
coverage — a thin statistical basis, carried as such.)

Full detail in `runs/wang_carbon_challenge.json`.

## 3. Discriminating observables — the matrix that lets the data decide

`hypothesis_discriminants.csv` scores 10 hypotheses × 10 observables. Each cell is flagged **DIAGNOSTIC** (a
signature that separates this hypothesis from the field), **DEGENERATE** (shared with others), or **NULL** (no
signal / wrong sign). Ranked by discriminating power among the leading viable hypotheses:

| Observable | Instrument(s) | Diagnostic for | Power |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S abundance** | MESSENGER XRS; BepiColombo **MIXS** (XRF, S Kα 2.308 keV, top ~10 µm) | separates all S-phases from chloride/carbon/non-volatile | **most decisive (5)** |
| **Local exosphere** | **PHEBUS** (UV, 55–315 nm); **SERENA/Strofio** (NMS) | S/S₂ (sulfur), Cl+Na (chloride photodesorption), Na/K (alkali sulfide) over *active* hollows | **decisive (4)** |
| **Cl, Na abundance** | **MIXS** (Cl Kα 2.622 keV / Na Kα 1.041 keV) | chloride (Cl+residual-Na anomaly) vs all S-phases | **decisive (3)** |
| **UV spectra** | MASCS UVVS; PHEBUS | photodesorption products; fullerene bands | strong (3) |
| depth / morphology, thermal inertia | MDIS, SIMBIO-SYS stereo; MERTIS radiometer | separates the non-volatile alternatives (melt = high TI; optical-only = zero relief) | moderate (2 each) |
| **7–14 µm emissivity** | **MERTIS TIS** | — | **degenerate (1)** |
| **high reflectance** | MDIS; SIMBIO-SYS | — | **degenerate (0)** |

**The central observational conclusion, reconfirmed and now made explicit:** composition (S, then Cl/Na) and the
**local exosphere** are the diagnostic axes; **thermal-IR emissivity and reflectance are degenerate** across
essentially every hypothesis. This is fully consistent with the MERTIS synthesis (no in-window volatile band; a
greenhouse contrast ratio identical for sulfur and halite at r=0.997). **The phase is identified by MIXS/XRS
composition and PHEBUS/Strofio exosphere, not by MERTIS.** The referee's SEALS-vs-SCArFS test (low-S vs high-S
hollows) is therefore an **XRF/exosphere** measurement, not a thermal-IR one.

## 4. Spatial-association tests (clustering-preserving null)

Hollows cluster in fields, so an independence null (latitude-matched random longitude) treats 2,518 correlated
points as independent, understates variance, and manufactures significance. All conclusions below use the
conservative **rigid-longitude-rotation** null, which preserves both the clustering and the latitude distribution.

- **Vents — clean negative (PI result, inherited).** No near-field enrichment at any radius 25–200 km; at 25 km the
  observed fraction (0.24%) is *below* the null mean (0.86%). A fumarolic/exhalative **point source is not required
  and not supported** as the general mechanism.
- **Craters — significant near-field association (this track).** Under the rotation null, hollows are concentrated
  within crater structures: at **5 km, 7.2% observed vs 3.0% null (z=3.4, p=0.008)**; at **10 km, 22.8% vs 11.4%
  (z=4.0, p=0.001)**; converging to the null beyond 25 km. The independence null inflates z to 12–18 at the same
  radii — the PI's methodological point, confirmed. This is the geologic counterpart of the vent negative: hollows
  associate with **impact structures** (walls, rims, central peaks), not with volcanic vents.
- **LRM — cite Wang, not the global raster.** The coarse 0.12°/px global "concentrated LRM" raster cannot support an
  association under the rotation null (z=−0.6). This does **not** refute the literature LRM association, which rests
  on higher-resolution local mapping; Wang et al. (2020) report hollows on LRM **excavated from depth** at local
  scale — the appropriate citation.

Together: a **concentrated, recently exposed crustal volatile-bearing body, unroofed by impacts** — consistent with
the pore-space concentration requirement (§5), the crater association, the vent non-association, and the Wang
~100 kyr / layer-thickness-limited depth distribution.

## 5. Referee-objection audit — adversarial, not reassuring

`referee_objection_audit.json` tracks 16 substantive objections extracted from the 2021 Icarus reviews (Reviewer 1
inline PDF comments + both general responses). **9 answered, 5 partially answered, 2 open.**

**Answered:**
- **Melting on the dayside** — refuted by the triple-point argument (Mercury at ~10⁻¹⁰ Pa sits ~9 orders below the
  S₈ triple point; no liquid field; the constraint is kinetic, not a phase change).
- **Vapor pressure is not an ambient pressure** — correct; P_v is a free-sublimation *rate ceiling*; steady-state loss
  is set by *supply*. Handled throughout the replenished-reservoir treatment.
- **Pore-space vs load-bearing fabric** — quantified: surface lowering requires the volatile to exceed
  x_v* ≈ 1 − φ_c/φ_total ≈ **4% of solid volume** (bracket 0–27%). The former must be **concentrated** (lens/vein/
  layer), not dispersed trace — now a hard screening filter.
- **Non-thermal loss ignored** — this is the study's central advance: nine athermal rate laws screened for every
  candidate.
- **Fullerene sublimation validity + volume** — matched: C₆₀/C₇₀ retained as viable-but-source-limited, and now
  rate-excluded under the Wang window.
- Plus: pits-vs-hollows morphology, the noon-below-predawn temperature artifact, the 10-m-model-depth concern, and
  the BepiColombo SEALS-vs-SCArFS discrimination target.

**Open (flagged for the geochemistry track, adversarially):**
- **Nāthair Facula S/C depletion** (XRS/NS show S and C *lost to space* in explosive eruptions) — weakens a juvenile-
  volcanic supply narrative and pushes the reservoir toward exospheric/ionic *recharge* (Verkercke 2025). Does not
  change the physics verdicts but must be addressed in the supply budget.
- **Effusive (smooth-plains) volcanism neglected in the S budget** — would enlarge the crustal S reservoir; a source-
  inventory question, not a loss-physics one.

**Partially answered** (carried forward, stated plainly): volatile purity/abundance for a *static* pure layer (the
concentration mechanism is invoked but not independently demonstrated); the decoupling of hollow age from host-crater
age (a model assumption, now supported by Wang's independent <103 kyr age but not yet an independent measurement per
field); the sulfide spectral mismatch (Lucchetti 2018 — consistent with sulfides as reservoir/residue, but a full VIRS
re-fit is in the MESSENGER reassessment plan); lateral growth; and the superseded 2021 density equation.

## 6. Bottom line for the study

No non-volatile mechanism and no athermal route can excavate Mercury's hollows on the Wang ~100 kyr timescale. The
non-volatile alternatives fail on **rate** (erosion, 5.9–7.8 orders short across the Wang bracket), **age** (impact-melt collapse), or **direct
refutation** (optical-only vs measured relief). Under the Wang window the athermal rehabilitation of the chlorides —
the study's prior headline — is converted into a **stronger negative**: an entire mechanism class is eliminated. What
survives every filter is **supply-limited thermal sublimation of elemental sulfur**, exposed by impacts and plausibly
maintained by exospheric/ionic replenishment. The concentration demand it must still meet is an **enrichment-or-depth
trade-off** (§2b): the sulfur is either locally enriched well above the planetary average, or fed from a reservoir
extending some hundreds of meters below the hollow through impact fractures — two co-equal branches the volatile
budget cannot separate, the second consistent with the crater association and vent non-association found here. The
phase is testable — but by **composition (MIXS/XRS S, Cl/Na) and exosphere (PHEBUS, SERENA/Strofio)**, not by
thermal-IR emissivity, which is degenerate.
